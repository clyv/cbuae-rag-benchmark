These are the 100 benchmark questions. Everything below is what the system actually returned when run over the corpus — real citations, real relevance scores, nothing mocked up. Because each question has labelled evidence, you can also see whether it found what a human said was required.

Why quotes and not prose? Because a quote cannot say something its source does not. The same pipeline was run through a local generative model to measure what paraphrasing costs: it declined 69 of the 100 questions, and of the claims it did write, only 0.557 could be traced back to the section it cited, against 0.972 when it quotes instead. That is a small model and the figure is a floor, not a verdict on generation — but it is why the default here quotes.

Why you cannot read the article text here. The CBUAE website terms permit downloading regulation for non-commercial use but not republishing it, and expressly permit direct links instead. So this public page shows which provision answers each question, how confident the system was, and whether it matched the labelled answer — then links you to the Central Bank's own page for the words. In place of the article text each required citation carries the labeller's note on why that provision answers the question, which is the part you cannot get by clicking through.

When should it refuse to answer?

Fourteen questions have no answer in this corpus. A system that answers them anyway is confidently wrong. Declining below a relevance score is the obvious fix — move the control and watch what it costs.

What was built, in six phases

Each phase produced a measurement rather than a claim. Full write-ups, with the raw output behind every number: retrieval · abstention · the price of fluency · the graph · the scope check · the planted-error control · the repository

1–2 · Corpus

46 insurance instruments, 763 sections, parsed from the Rulebook's own HTML rather than from PDFs.

Section identity is citable, which is what makes evidence scoreable at all. Three registry rows initially fetched a whole category — catching that stopped one article being indexed under four document ids.

3 · Benchmark

100 questions across six categories, each labelled with the sections that must be found to answer it.

Deliberate errors were planted in the answer key to test the review process: 5 of 8 caught, so the benchmark is reported as holding at most about three label errors rather than claimed clean.

4 · Retrieval

Four architectures behind one interface, measured on identical inputs with paired bootstrap intervals.

Hybrid + reranking 0.750 against BM25's 0.581. At 50 questions the same comparison could not be established at all.

5 · Answering

Extractive answers, citation validation, and abstention driven by the reranker's score.

Citations are grounded 1.000 by construction. Abstention separates answerable from unanswerable at AUC 0.806 (95% interval 0.671–0.915).

6 · Cross-reference graph

Regulation cites itself constantly, so following those citations should recover evidence the search missed. 193 references resolved to 102 edges.

It changes nothing: recall@10 0.750 either way, interval +0.000 to +0.000, and not one question moved.

× · Scope check

The hardest refusals are questions about entities the corpus does not govern. Every instrument states who it binds, so scoring the question against that should catch them.

Worse than relevance alone: 0.806 falls to 0.675 when scope is added.

Why the graph failed, which is the interesting part

Following a citation only helps if the provisions a question needs are the ones the text links. 59 questions require exactly two sections — and 2 of those 59 pairs are joined by an explicit reference. Before building anything, the ceiling said the same: a flawless expansion could rescue one of the 35 questions the system misses evidence on, and more hops do not help because the graph runs out of edges.

Cross-references encode procedural dependency — do this in accordance with that provision. A question needs topical completeness: the two rules that together answer it. Two rules about capital adequacy answer a question together and have no reason to cite one another, because neither is a step in the other's procedure. The structure the corpus writes down is not the structure the questions need.
